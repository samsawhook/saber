The Stakes

A failed executive search is a line item no one budgets for.

Recruiting cost, severance, lost momentum, and the second search — a mis-hire at the executive level routinely costs a multiple of first-year compensation. Most failures trace back to the same three process gaps.

01

Networks are narrow

Traditional search draws on who a firm already knows. The strongest candidate for your role is usually in no one’s rolodex — and is not applying to anything.

02

Assessment is unstructured

Interviews alone predict very little. Without consistent evidence — credentials verified, outcomes referenced, criteria scored — decisions default to instinct.

03

Cycles run long

Every month of vacancy in a finance or legal seat carries real cost: delayed closes, audit exposure, stalled initiatives, and a team managing up instead of forward.

The Tandem Method

Human judgment and machine precision, interwoven.

Neither replaces the other. Software cannot close a candidate or read a boardroom; people cannot read a whole market. Our method is built on both strands — and on the discipline of keeping them honest.

The human strand

  • Partner-led from the first briefing to the signed offer — the person you meet is the person who runs the search.
  • Confidential outreach that senior executives actually answer.
  • Structured interviews and reference work done by people who have sat in the seats they assess.
  • Negotiation, resignation, and onboarding managed through the first year.

The machine strand

  • Full-market mapping — every qualified executive in scope, not a sample of the available ones.
  • Credential and license verification at scale: CPA, CFA, JD, professional registrations.
  • Calibrated scoring against your criteria — the same rubric applied to every candidate.
  • Weekly reporting you can audit, not anecdotes you have to take on faith.

How a Search Runs

Six weeks to a shortlist you can defend.

  1. 01Week 1

    Calibrate

    We sit with you and your stakeholders to define the role in evidence: outcomes expected, credentials required, the criteria a finalist must score against. You approve the rubric before we contact anyone.

  2. 02Weeks 1–3

    Map

    Meridian builds the full market — everyone qualified, not everyone available. Your partner reviews and prioritizes; confidential outreach begins.

  3. 03Weeks 3–6

    Assess

    Structured interviews, credential verification, and referencing against the rubric. You receive a shortlist with the evidence attached — not just our opinion of it.

  4. 04Offer → Year one

    Secure

    Your partner manages negotiation, resignation, and transition, then stays with the placement through the first year. Retention is a commitment we report on, not a hope.

Straight Answers

The questions CFOs ask us first.

What does a search cost?

We work on a retained basis with a fixed, agreed fee — no surprises tied to compensation negotiations. The full fee structure goes in writing before you commit to anything.

What happens if the placement doesn’t work out?

Every search carries a replacement assurance: if a placement departs within the guarantee period, we re-run the search at no additional professional fee. The exact terms are in the engagement letter.

How is AI actually used — and where isn’t it?

Meridian does the reading: mapping the market, structuring evidence, keeping scoring consistent. It does not make decisions. Every candidate you meet was interviewed, referenced, and recommended by a partner accountable to you by name.

How do you handle confidentiality?

Searches are confidential by default. Outreach never names the client without written approval, sensitive searches run under a mandate code, and candidate data is access-controlled and handled on a need-to-know basis.

Why not use our internal recruiters, or a contingency firm?

For most roles, you should use internal recruiting. Retained search earns its fee where the seat is critical, the market is thin, and the strongest candidates aren’t applying. That is the only work we take.
